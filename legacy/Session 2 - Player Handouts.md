# Session 2 — Player Handouts & Props

*Physical props to hand the players (mostly Katara). Each has a GM note on when to give it. Print them, or copy the text onto index cards / scrap paper. To make them feel real: a script/handwritten font, torn or tea-stained edges, and (for the burned note) actually singe the paper edges.*

*🎨 Art later: each prop has an **[Art:]** line describing an illustration we can generate when you want to do the visual pass.*

---

## 0. The Broadsheet ("Midwinter Mayhem")
**When:** Beat 0, when Perrin Oglethorpe shows up in the bookshop before Clarence arrives. Hand it over as soon as he unfolds it — let the players read the exaggerations themselves and react.
**How to present:** a cheap one-copper newsprint sheet, already a little worn from passing through several hands before it reached Perrin's. Rendered version, with the woodcut illustration embedded: [handouts_html/broadsheet.html](handouts_html/broadsheet.html). Standalone illustration if you just want the image: [art/handouts/horse_hydra_woodcut.png](art/handouts/horse_hydra_woodcut.png).

**GM note:** this is the recap, dressed as a joke instead of a lecture. The wrong details (a **seven-headed horse hydra**, witnesses who can't agree if it's five heads or a dozen, "nine gingerbread carts") are the hook — let the players correct it out loud, and the true facts (the Horn, The Hawk, Clarence, the escape) surface as their own bragging rather than your exposition. It also plants "gossip travels fast," which pays off when Mrs. Claus begs for no headlines in Beat 2.

**Bonus prop:** there's also a proper chibi character-art version of the "horse hydra," matching the rest of the campaign's art style, at [art/horse_hydra.png](art/horse_hydra.png) — a pile of tiny horse heads all beaming at once. Good for a laugh if you want to reveal "what the papers pictured vs. what Clarence actually looks like" as a sight gag later.

## 1. Santa's Burned Note
**When:** Beat 3 (Santa's Office), found in the fireplace grate. The single most important clue — it flips "Santa is the thief" into "Santa is hiding to protect us."
**How to present:** the paper is charred; whole words are burned away. Read only what survives; let the players fill the gaps.

> *[Art: a scorched half-sheet of heavy cream paper, elegant looping handwriting, the bottom third burned to black curling ash.]*
>
> ```
>   ...can't trust the log. Someone inside opened the
>   way for it. The mo̶t̶h̶ knows the Star is here and it
>   will not stop —
>
>   Moved the Star to the old [·······burned·······]
>   where no one goes anymore. If you're reading this
>   and I'm not back: DON'T put it on the sleigh until
>   you're certain who [······burned······]
>
>   — trust no one on the Bo[·······burned·······]
> ```

**GM note:** the burned word after "the old ___" is *Coal & Complaints wing* (Judy's memory or Pib fills it in). The burned "Bo—" is *Board* (the VPs), pointing suspicion at the leadership without naming Noelle. Hand this over and *stop talking* — let them read it twice.

## 2. Noelle's Torn Journal Page
**When:** Beat 5, found in Noelle's workspace once they've reason to search it (or on her, if cornered). This is the emotional gut-punch clue — a 12-year-old should read it and feel *sad*, not just "gotcha."
**How to present:** a crumpled, much-folded page in small, careful handwriting. **It's a diary, not a manifesto** — she isn't making a case to anyone, she's writing things down because there's nobody to say them to.

> *[Art: a crumpled lined page, tiny neat pencil handwriting, one corner torn, a faint smudge like a tear dried on the ink.]*
>
> *"Nobody said thank you again today. I'm not writing that down to be dramatic. I just wanted it written down somewhere.*
>
> *Fourteen years. I know every child's list by heart. Mira in Waterdeep has asked for the same dog three years running. I fixed the toy line three times and Fig signed all three.*
>
> *Nobody here uses my name. They say "the coordinator," like I'm a clipboard that learned to walk. Last week a form came through with COORDINATOR printed where the name goes. I filled it in anyway.*
>
> *Then somebody talked to me, down by the returns pile where they keep the toys nobody picked. He knew my name before I said it. He said the ones who get left out could stop getting left out, and that I could be the one who did it.*
>
> *I know it isn't right. I keep waiting to feel that. Mostly what I feel is picked.*
>
> *I told the reindeer I was sorry. Out loud. Which is a silly thing to do when there's nobody listening.*
>
> *A better Christmas. That's all I wanted. Just to be the one who—"* (the page is torn off here)

**GM note:** this is the clue AND the seed of the mercy beat. If any player reads it aloud, the redemption ending in Beat 6 lands ten times harder. **Two lines are doing the real work:** *"Fig signed all three"* is the whole grievance in four words, and *"I told the reindeer I was sorry"* is a fair, catchable confession that never states the crime — a kid will figure out what she's apologizing for about a second after they finish reading, and that beat of dawning is worth more than a checklist. Don't explain either one.

**Wren note (if you're running the third PC):** if the bard's player is in the room when this gets read, look at them. Noelle's page is a person writing down that nobody sees them, and Wren said almost exactly that in a bookshop in Beat 0. You don't have to point it out — but if that player wants to reach for the mercy ending later, this is the moment they'll remember.

## 3. The Moth-Letter (from The Hawk)
**When:** Beat 5, tucked with the torn page (or hidden behind the returns shelf). Confirms an *outside* master and drops the first breadcrumb of the gauntlet arc.
**How to present:** fine, eerie, elegant handwriting on grey paper, sealed with a wax **moth** instead of a stamp. Cold and gentle at once.

> *[Art: a folded slip of ash-grey paper, silver ink, a broken wax seal shaped like a moth with spread wings; faint iridescent dust clinging to the fold.]*
>
> *"Little coordinator —*
>
> *You did beautifully with the herd. They never even looked at the returns pile, and neither will the ones the horse sent.*
>
> *Now the small one with the light. He is the only one of them that matters and he will not come easily — the others took what I sent them in their sleep, but he will refuse it from anywhere but a hand. So it must be your hand, and it must be close, and you must not stop when he shies. Turn him and he will show us where the Star is; he cannot help it any more than he can help shining.*
>
> *Bring me the Star and you will never be overlooked again — I give you my word, and my word is the only thing I have left.*
>
> *The Star is only one of six. I have carried the first myself for longer than your workshop has stood, and it is heavy in a way you would not survive. When the ring is full, I will set right the thing that was taken from me, and the whole world will be remade kinder. You will have earned your place in it.*
>
> *Keep the brooch close. It is listening.*
>
> *— H."*

**GM note (the Rudolph paragraph — this one they CAN decode, and should):** unlike the rest of the letter, the second paragraph is a plain-language warning of exactly what's about to happen: he wants the small glowing one, it has to be done by hand, and a turned Rudolph points at the Star. **If the players find this letter before Beat 6, they can run for the stables and arrive during the attempt instead of after it starts** — same scene, but they're the ones who made it in time, which is a much better feeling. Don't hint; just let the letter say it and see if they move. (If they don't, nothing is lost — Beat 6 happens on schedule either way.)

**GM note:** three further teasers, none of which the players can decode yet — which is the point. "Only one of six" is the gauntlet. "I have carried the first myself" is the **Vial**. "Heavy in a way you would not survive" is the grief he poured into it and hasn't felt since. And "keep the brooch close, it is listening" quietly plants the boss weak-point for anyone paying attention. If a player asks what "six" means, Noelle doesn't know either — *"He never explained. I didn't ask."*

## 4. Judy's Detective Notebook (Katara's worksheet)
**When:** Hand to Katara at the very start (Beat 0/1). It's her play-along sheet — somewhere to write clues and guesses, which keeps a 12-year-old *active* through the talky bits.
**How to present:** print it as a fillable page (or just draw the boxes on paper). Let her scribble.

> ```

**GM note:** listing Noelle in the suspect box (as "guide") is deliberate — it plants, fairly, that even the helper *could* be a suspect, without you saying a word.

**GM note (the important one):** the **"who wasn't in the room"** box is the single best piece of paper in this session. It is Katara's on-ramp to the actual solution — Noelle slips away before every reindeer turns, and this worksheet asks a 12-year-old to write down that name four times without ever telling her why. **Hand it over at the start and never mention it again.** If she fills it in, she solves the mystery herself, and it will be the best thing that happens all night.

**Third-player note:** if you're running Wren (the bard), don't print them a second worksheet — it's Judy's prop and Katara's spotlight. Give Wren the **herd names** as their own thing to care about instead; they're the one who learns the calling song, and "which reindeer are still standing" is a question they'll ask without being prompted.

## 6. A Page of the Naughty & Nice List
**When:** Beat 4/5 via Pib's records — a fun clue prop, a table full of in-jokes, and a callback to the climax's "put her on the Naughty List" ending.
**How to present:** an official-looking ledger page, dense with names, half of them people at the table. **Hand it over and shut up** — let them find their own names, and let them find the crossed-out one on their own too. Printable version: [handouts_html/naughty_nice_list.html](handouts_html/naughty_nice_list.html).

> *[Art: a tall ledger page, two ruled columns "NICE" and "NAUGHTY," one Nice-side name recently crossed out and re-written on the Naughty side in a different hand, the ink still wet.]*

**THE LIST — Vol. MMXXIV** · Records: P. Quillby · *page 1,407 of 9,412 (this volume)*

| ✦ NICE | ✗ NAUGHTY |
|---|---|
| **Katara F.** — asked for nothing. Receiving extra. | **Nyx Ellywick** — set fire to it. Records declines to specify what; there wasn't room. |
| **Gabrielle A.** — see attached commendation (3 pp.) | **Oliver Greycastle** — *see Olive.* |
| **Felix** — third consecutive year. Records assumed clerical error, investigated, and apologized. | **Olive Greycastle** — *see Oliver.* |
| **Squish** — is a good one. That is the entire entry. | **Durnan** — watered it down. He knows what he did. |
| **Lehmohn** — shared. Unprompted. Twice. | **Joel** — knows what he did. Continues to do it. |
| **Ambrosia** — gave it back before anyone noticed it was gone. | **Geoff** — *"it was ONE time"* (×14) |
| **Mayellah Echomyst** — wrote apology letters nobody had asked for. | **Fig T.** — ambition is not technically a crime. Entry retained anyway. |
| **Paris** — held the door for eleven consecutive people. | *(that one kid who bit the mall Santa)* |
| **Levi** — did the washing-up unasked. Flagged as suspicious. Reviewed. Upheld. | *(whoever keeps taking the last biscuit and putting the empty tin back)* |
| **Priscilla** — talked somebody out of a genuinely terrible idea. | *(everyone who says "actually" to a person who was already correct)* |
| **Francine** — fed a stray something warm. | *(a goose. Records stands by this.)* |
| **Kristina** — fed the same stray. Coordination denied. | *(the individual who reclines on a two-hour flight)* |
| **Marlowe** — has never once been unkind on purpose. | ~~─────────────────~~ |
| **Bramble F.** — does not believe it. Entry stands. | **N. Brightbough** — ?? |
| **Clarence** — species review pending; conduct exemplary. | |
| **Perrin O.** — provisional, pending fewer questions. | |
| ~~**N. Brightbough** ✦~~ → *moved* | |
| *(the elf who apologised to a chair he walked into)* | |

**GM note:** the wet-ink move of *N. Brightbough* from Nice to Naughty is a fair, catchable pointer at Noelle before the reveal — and if the players don't clock it, it's a satisfying "ohhh" in hindsight. **The load-bearing detail is the handwriting:** the crossing-out on the Nice side and the re-entry on the Naughty side are in a **different hand from every other entry on the page**, and the Naughty-side ink is still tacky. Pib didn't write it. *Nobody* wrote it — the List did, tonight, on its own, because it knows. That's much creepier than a clerk with a grudge, and it's the same magic that lets Santa's justice land at the end.

**Also worth knowing:** if a player asks *who* moved it, the honest answer from Pib is a very unsettled *"...nobody moves the List."* Let that sit.

# Writing for Katara's campaign

Everything written here gets read at a table or scanned mid-session by a busy DM. Write for that. Two standards apply to every piece of prose: sound like a person, and run like DM prep. `CLAUDE.md` carries the short version.

## Part 1: Kill the AI tells

The DM can spot machine-written prose and does not want it.

### The em-dash. Hard rule: do not use it.
No `--`, no `&mdash;`, no the long dash character. It is the loudest tell. Rewrite every place you would reach for one:
- A hard break becomes a full stop. "Santa is gone and so is the Star" works as two sentences.
- An aside becomes a comma pair or parentheses. "Fig, who has never once said thank you, signs the form."
- A lead-in to a list or payoff becomes a colon. "Three things are wrong tonight: the bells are silent, the reindeer are foaming, and the boss has vanished."
If a sentence only holds together with a dash, it is two sentences or it wants a comma.

### The "not X, but Y" reflex. Cut it.
*"Not a monster, but a collector." "It isn't about the money, it's about trust."* One real antithesis in a whole document is fine. A pattern of them is the tell. Say what a thing is and stop.

### The rule of three. Break it.
Do not stack three adjectives, three list items, or three parallel clauses by reflex. Vary the count: one strong word, two items, four items.

### Dead vocabulary. Never use.
delve, tapestry, testament to, a symphony of, a dance of, nestled, bustling, vibrant or rich as filler, boasts, whimsical, navigate the complexities, at its core, in a world where, it's worth noting, ultimately, in summary, that said, and sweeping "whether X or Y" or "from X to Y" frames.

### Trailing "-ing" tails. Prune them.
Avoid ending a sentence with "..., ensuring the elves stay calm" or "..., making it a memorable moment." End on the concrete thing. Start a new sentence if the consequence matters.

### Throat-clearing and over-balance.
No windups ("It's important to understand that..."), no sentence that restates the paragraph. Vary sentence length. Short ones are fine. Fragments are fine.

## Part 2: Write like DM prep

### Read-aloud (boxed text)
- **Short.** Two to four sentences, then hand it back with "what do you do?"
- **Two or three senses, sound and smell early.** Not all five.
- **Second person, present tense.** "You push the shop door open. The bell above it clunks instead of ringing."
- **One ordinary anchor.** A dented mug, a sticky floor, wet wool. The ordinary detail makes the fantastic real.
- **Never bury a clue in prose.** If they must notice it, it gets its own short beat.
- **End on a hook or an image.** Cut on the wrong detail or the thing unsaid.
- **Say it out loud first.** If you stumble, rewrite.

### DM notes
- **Front-load the usable thing:** the trigger, the DC, the number, the reveal.
- **Bullets and bold keys over paragraphs.** Prose is for read-aloud.
- **Separate** read-aloud, mechanics, and secrets into labeled blocks.
- **Secrets as a list of facts**, revealable in any order.
- **Every NPC gets a line to say,** one voice tic, one physical tic, one want.
- **Options, not a script.** Never write what Judy, Dormir or Lyric choose, buy, say or feel. Describe the world and hand it over.

## Part 3: The reader at the table
- **Katara is 13, mature, smart and quick-witted.** Write for a sharp reader. Plain words, no talking down, no cutesy narration.
- **Humor:** dry, sarcastic, deadpan, and chaotic all work. NPCs can be snarky. Bureaucratic elves, unreliable reindeer, and a horse pretending to be a unicorn are the house style. The humor comes from characters, not from the world being a joke.
- **Her interests are winks, never fixtures.** A Pusheen-shaped biscuit, a vendor selling tiny scale-model shops, a Sanrio-style mascot on a mug. One per scene at most. Never build a scene around a borrowed franchise.
- **Puzzles and clues are the main attraction.** Make clues concrete and fair, and let her connect them.

## Tone compass (always)
About 50% mystery and clue-hunting, 30% comedy, 20% wonder. PG. Danger stays real but never grim. Land every eerie beat back in warmth.

## Self-check before you finish
1. Search the text for `--` and the long dash. Zero allowed.
2. Find every "not X, but Y" hinge. Keep at most one.
3. Count triads. Break the pattern.
4. Scan for dead vocabulary. Cut every hit.
5. Read every read-aloud block out loud. Cut anything past four sentences.
6. Confirm no PC choice was scripted.
